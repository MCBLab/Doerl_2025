Maturation of the glymphatic system confers innate resistance of the brain to Zika virus infection

Doerl, Chaves, dos Santos, Marcelino, Coelho, Miyakoshi, Araujo, Jeronimo & Sequerra. A maturing glymphatic system acts as an innate barrier to ZIKV: injected before maturation the virus spreads widely, deforms vessels and disrupts the Aqp4+ astrocyte network; after maturation the same challenge causes no disease. Blocking vasopressin receptor 1a reopens the window of susceptibility.

Macaque · single-cell

The astrocyte endfoot after infant Zika infection

A re-analysis of GEO GSE342497 (Edara et al., JCI Insight 2026): infant rhesus macaques infected at 4 weeks of age, past the equivalent of the mouse glymphatic maturation window. Tests AQP4 and the whole dystrophin–glycoprotein anchoring complex, its basement-membrane ligands, AVPR1A and the interferon response, per region and cell type. Finds the anchoring module coordinately lower in ZIKV astrocytes and pericytes while AQP4 itself is unchanged.

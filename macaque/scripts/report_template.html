Cross-study analysis

The astrocyte endfoot after infant Zika infection

A mouse study reports that a mature glymphatic system protects the brain from Zika virus, and that infecting before maturity disrupts the AQP4+ astrocyte network around vessels. This report asks what an independent macaque dataset — infants infected after the equivalent maturation point — says about the same machinery, gene by gene.

In short

What this re-analysis adds

  • The macaque data are consistent with a protected, mature brain. No viral reads, no leukocyte influx, and AQP4 transcript is unchanged in astrocytes — the cells that actually express it.
  • But the machinery that holds AQP4 at the vessel is lower. In amygdala and hippocampus astrocytes, dystroglycan (DAG1), KCNJ10, MLC1, UTRN, LARGE1 and the basement-membrane ligands NID1, LAMB2, LAMC1, HSPG2 and AGRN all fall together. The effect is strongest in GFAP-high astrocytes.
  • Pericytes lose the same basement-membrane transcripts, so this is a shared perivascular signature, not one cell type drifting.
  • A testable bridge. The mouse paper measures AQP4 coverage of vessels; this dataset shows the transcriptional anchoring module that would produce such a loss. Neither measures the other — which is exactly why AQP4 immunostaining in these macaque tissues is the obvious next experiment.
  • Caveat first, not last. Three animals versus two. Nothing here survives correction across the whole targeted panel. Treat every number as hypothesis-generating.

The same question one cell at a time

Everything above summed each animal's cells into one value per group. A cell-level model asks a different question: does the fraction of astrocytes expressing the anchor change? MAST fits a hurdle model that separates detection from level, with the cellular detection rate as a covariate.

The catch, and it is a big one. Cells from the same animal are not independent. Fitting MAST the conventional way — treating all 176,570 cells as replicates — produces P-values that are not interpretable here: DAG1 in astrocytes comes out at P = 3×10−295. Refitting the same model with the animal as a random effect moves it to P = 1.8×10−4. Across the endfoot genes, the median shift is 2.3 orders of magnitude, the largest is 291, and 18 of 58 cell-level "significant" results stop being significant altogether. Prior work

Is this actually one complex?

The argument of this report rests on these six genes being parts of one structure rather than six genes that happen to move together. They are. Each link below is established by loss-of-function work in brain or glia.

GeneRole in the anchorKey evidence
DAG1α/β-dystroglycan — binds laminin and agrin outside, dystrophin inside Laminin clusters Kir4.1 and AQP4 through a dystroglycan complex in astrocytes (Guadagno & Moukhles 2004); dystroglycan deletion alters endfoot membrane architecture (Noell 2011); glia-specific deletion separates the glial role (Satz 2010)
DMDDystrophin, glial isoform Dp71 — the inner scaffold Dystrophin loss reduces endfoot AQP4 (Frigeri 2001) and delays brain oedema (Vajda 2002); only the perivascular pool is dystrophin-dependent (Nicchia 2008); Dp71 loss depolarises AQP4 and impairs glymphatic function (Yang 2024)
UTRNUtrophin — dystrophin's paralogue, forms a distinct variant of the complex Utrophin and Dp71 assemble different complexes at brain microvasculature (Haenggi 2004); glia run their own composition (Blake 1999)
LARGE1Builds matriglycan, the sugar that lets dystroglycan bind the basement membrane Without the glycan, dystroglycan cannot bind laminin or agrin (Michele 2002); LARGE is the enzyme that makes it (Inamori 2012); LARGE loss disturbs astrocyte–vessel interaction (Zhou 2017)
SNTA1α-syntrophin — its PDZ domain binds the AQP4 C-terminus directly Required for AQP4 to reach the endfoot (Neely 2001); defines the perivascular AQP4 pool (Amiry-Moghaddam 2003a) and K+ clearance (2003b); the whole scaffold assembles as one unit (Bragg 2006)
DTNAα-dystrobrevin — binds syntrophin and dystrophin, stabilises the assembly Glial α-dystrobrevin loss causes AQP4 redistribution, barrier failure and progressive oedema (Lien 2012); part of the same perivascular scaffold (Bragg 2006)

Full citations with PMIDs and DOIs are in endfoot_anchor_references.md. The point this makes for the comparison: every one of these loss-of-function phenotypes is AQP4 present but mislocalised, not AQP4 absent — which is exactly the pattern here, where AQP4 transcript is flat while its anchor falls.

Caveats

  • Five animals. Three controls, two infected. Every P-value rests on that. No panel gene survives correction across the full targeted set; the FDR values shown are genome-wide within one cell type and region.
  • Infection is confounded with processing. Condition may coincide with batch, and nothing in the design separates them.
  • Ambient RNA is worse in the infected libraries. Vascular transcripts spill into every cell type: ACTA2 appears to rise 3–4 log2 in astrocytes and microglia, which barely express it, while rising only ~0.5 in the smooth-muscle cells that make it. Every change here is re-tested after subtracting an estimate of that foreign signal; those that do not survive carry †. The decreases in the anchoring module go in the opposite direction to ambient contamination, which is why they are the most credible signal.
  • Dp71 cannot be resolved. It is a short DMD isoform sharing the 3′ end that 10x chemistry captures. Only total DMD is measurable; separating Dp71 needs isoform-aware counting from raw reads.
  • Zero viral UMIs is not evidence of absence. No poly(A) tail, so the assay barely sees the genome.
  • Region labels in GEO are swapped (hippocampus ↔ striatum) relative to the file names. The file names are correct, and this analysis uses them; the marker evidence is in the repository README.
  • mRNA is not protein, and certainly not localisation. A fall in DAG1 transcript is a hypothesis about the endfoot, not a measurement of it.
If this is worth following

What would settle it

  • AQP4 and β-dystroglycan immunostaining on the archived macaque tissue, scored as perivascular polarisation rather than total signal. This is the direct test of whether the transcript module matters, and it is the same readout the mouse study already uses.
  • Laminin or collagen IV co-stain to ask whether the basement membrane itself is thinner where NID1 and LAMB2 are down.
  • Isoform-aware reanalysis from the raw reads to split Dp71 from full-length dystrophin — Dp71 is the isoform that anchors AQP4, and the current answer is blind to it.
  • Any CSF-tracer or contrast imaging in the macaque model. The preprint's authors call for exactly this in humans; the macaque is where it is feasible.
  • More animals. Everything else is secondary to this.
Reproducibility

Methods and files

Cells were called with emptyDrops and filtered on gene count, UMI count and mitochondrial fraction; doublets were removed with scDblFinder. Samples were integrated with Harmony and annotated from canonical markers, with neuronal sub-clustering. For differential expression, counts were summed per animal × region × cell type and tested with edgeR quasi-likelihood (~ condition) on the full transcriptome, separately for each region and cell type, requiring at least two animals per arm with at least 10 cells each. The ambient-RNA correction estimates, for each tested group, the foreign contribution from every other cell type using that type's specific markers, and re-computes the fold change after subtracting it.
